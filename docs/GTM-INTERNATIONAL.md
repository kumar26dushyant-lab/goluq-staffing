# GoLuQ.com — International go-to-market (UAE, Australia, New Zealand, US, UK)

Written 2026-09-11. Goal set by the founder: **one custom software or app
delivery every month at a high ticket, with a six-month pipeline**, with
international clients first and India second. WhatsApp configuration stays in
the catalogue as the wedge; the money is in custom software, apps and
automation agents.

Sources: Qubit Labs, Apptunix, eSparkInfo, Aalpha, Uvik (offshore rate
guides 2026); Message Central, SleekFlow, GoDesign, Innovatrix (UAE WhatsApp
and AI automation pricing 2026); SoftTeco, Azilen, ADEVS, Hudasoft (AI agent

**Reading of the table.** A local agency in Sydney, Dubai or London charges
three to eight times what GoLuQ needs to charge to hit the revenue goal. An
Indian body shop charges less than GoLuQ but sells hours, teams and account
managers. The gap in the middle — fixed price, one accountable person who
also runs the WhatsApp and phone lines, a first release in 21 days, and the
customer owns the code — is the position. Price at roughly 40–60% below the
local agency, two to three times the body shop, and never by the hour.

## 2. The offer, stated the way a buyer reads it

- **Discovery sprint, $900 (credited to the build).** One week: the owner's
  problem on paper, a blueprint, a fixed quote. Removes the "how much" fear
  and filters tyre-kickers.
- **App or software build, $9,900–$24,900 fixed.** 21-day first release,
  then stages. Own the code.
- **Multi-branch platform, $19,900–$49,900 in stages.** Nidaan is the proof.
- **Automation agent for one workflow, $7,900–$19,900.** Priced per workflow,
  the model and API bills passed through at cost.
- **WhatsApp Office / Store, $2,900 + $490/mo.** The wedge, and the reason a
  small trader can start.
- **Managed, $490/mo.** The relationship that produces the next project.

Average target ticket: **$15,000**. One a month is the founder's goal;
pipeline maths below assumes a 20% close rate from proposal.

## 3. Channels, ranked by expected yield for this profile

1. **LinkedIn, founder-led.** The buyer is an owner or a GM of a 5–50
   person firm. They buy from a person. Profile rebuilt around the story
   (headline: "I build the software your business runs on — and the WhatsApp
   and phone lines that connect it to customers. Fixed price. You own it.").
   Three posts a week: one chapter of the homepage story as a 20-second
   clip, one "how we built it" (Nidaan, EagleEye, Sarathi), one plain
   opinion post about renting vs owning software. 25 personalised connection
   requests a day to owners in the five countries and the five verticals
   below; no pitch in the request; the pitch is the profile and the posts.
2. **Marketplaces for the first international proofs.** Upwork (US, AU, UK
   small businesses hire there; fixed-price contracts with milestones map to
   the offer) and a Clutch profile with three verified reviews (Ashwin/Nidaan,
   the Dubai client after delivery, one more). These exist to make the first
   three international invoices happen and to produce reviews; they are not
   the long-term channel.
3. **Partners.** The Dubai client's circle (garment traders across the Gulf,
   Iran, CIS, Romania, Hungary) on the profit-share programme; Indian-diaspora
   business associations in Dubai, Sydney, Auckland, London; chartered
   accountants and business consultants who see owners' pain first. The
   partner programme already pays on profit; give partners the story link
   with `?r=` for their region and the chapter share buttons.
4. **Verticals, not geographies.** Same five stories, sold to the same five
   kinds of owner everywhere: coaching/training, clinics, wholesale/trading,
   claims/legal/accounting firms, multi-branch service businesses. Each gets a
   landing page (`/for/<vertical>`) that is the chapter plus a case, in the
   region's words and currency.
5. **Short video.** The six chapters cut as 9:16 clips for Instagram, YouTube
   Shorts, LinkedIn and Facebook, with the founder's face on the intro (real
   phone video, not the avatar, for trust). Content, not ads, for the first
   90 days.
6. **Search, small and precise.** Google Ads on long-tail intent only
   ("custom CRM for insurance claims firm", "WhatsApp ordering system for
   wholesalers"), $10–20/day per country, landing on the vertical pages.
   Turn off anything that does not produce a booked call in 30 days.
7. **Email, compliant.** UK B2B and US business email is allowed with a clear
   opt-out; Australia and New Zealand require consent (Spam Act 2003,
   Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act 2007) — so no cold email there; use
   LinkedIn and partners instead. Sequences of three short emails with one
   case each, one ask: a 30-minute call.

  DMs.
- Post three times (assets from me).

  from the catalogue, from me).

Monthly
- One case study published (from the delivered project).
- Review the funnel report; kill what did not produce a call.

## 7. What not to do

- No hourly rates on the site. No "starting from $5/hour" positioning.
- No paid ads before the vertical pages and the videos exist.
- No cold email to Australia or New Zealand.
- No claims we cannot show: only Nidaan's published numbers and delivered
  work.
